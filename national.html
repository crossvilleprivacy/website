Spotlight · Brookfield, Wisconsin · August 6, 2026 · stale Flock homicide alert / high-risk stop

Brookfield PD holds SUV occupants at gunpoint after an outdated Milwaukee Flock alert

On August 6, 2026, around 4:46 p.m., a Flock Safety camera flagged a vehicle on Interstate 94 in Waukesha County as linked to a Milwaukee homicide with a suspect believed to be armed. CBS 58 reported that Brookfield officers then ran a high-risk stop on westbound I-94 and ordered two people out of the SUV at gunpoint. Milwaukee PD had already cleared the vehicle from the investigation but had left the alert live in Flock. An MPD spokesperson told local stations, including TMJ4, “This was not a Flock issue. The occupants were released after Milwaukee confirmed it no longer wanted the car or the people.

ACLU of Wisconsin analyst Jon McCray Jones, quoted by CBS 58, called the episode a systemic failure: shared Flock data led another jurisdiction to stop an innocent person at gunpoint. No lawsuit is reported in the sources cited here as of August 11, 2026.

Brown County, Wisconsin · April 2025 stop · Flock interstate travel / cannabis pretext

404 Media: deputies used Flock to follow a plate across state lines, then treated legal Michigan travel as weed-search fodder

404 Media reviewed a Brown County criminal complaint alleging deputies used the shared Flock network to follow a driver’s plate from Wisconsin into Michigan and back on April 26, 2025. Roseville, California · July 31, 2026 · Flock alert accuracy / plate misreads

Roseville PD found Flock misread plates in 71% of stolen or felony alerts

Business Insider’s Nicole Einbinder reported on July 31, 2026 that Roseville police analyzed Flock alerts from 2023 and 2024 and found the system incorrectly read plates in 71% of the 1,427 alerts flagging vehicles as stolen or used in a felony. Records also describe repeated character swaps (for example, 9 as 8), blurry or delayed captures, and missed vehicles. Roseville said none of those incorrect alerts led to a stop or arrest because officers must verify a plate before stopping; California has no statewide verify-before-stop rule like some other states.

Flock told BI its character-read accuracy exceeds 96% in optimal conditions and blamed Roseville’s atypical rear-only camera setup and older hardware placements. Roseville disputed that performance has clearly improved and said it still meets with Flock twice a month. NYU Policing Project’s Max Isaacs called a 71% misread rate on those alerts “beyond the pale.” This site also cites Roseville’s very low EFF hit ratio (about 0.03% of scans matching wanted lists), a different metric from alert-level OCR error rates.

National · July to August 2026 · Mother Jones / Washington Post / IJ stalking roundup

Washington Post and IJ map a national stalker-cop pattern on Flock

A July 2026 Mother Jones report summarized the romantic-partner stalking pattern across Flock / ALPR systems: Milwaukee’s Ayala case, then IA Det. Tehrangi Chapman charged while investigating that same case, plus firings and charges in Illinois, South Carolina, Texas, California, and Georgia. The Institute for Justice catalog cited in that piece identified at least 24 similar ALPR stalking cases in two years, nearly all ending in criminal charges and job loss.

On August 2, 2026, the Washington Post published a police-and-court-records investigation finding at least 50 officers accused, charged, or convicted of using license-plate readers for unauthorized purposes. Flock’s system was used in 46 of those cases. In 26, investigators said the targets were people in the officer’s personal life. The lead case is Braselton, Georgia, Police Chief Michael Steffman; the Post reported roughly 600 lookups of a civilian. After publication, follow-up coverage said the Post confirmed more than a dozen additional cases. Those figures are a national investigation’s count, not this site’s Case_ID tally (this catalog has 69 misuse rows, including cases the Post may not have listed and excluding some the Post counted).

On August 12, the Institute for Justice launched a searchable ALPR-abuse database. IJ says it catalogues more than 100 incidents of romantic stalking, wrongful stops and detentions, non-law-enforcement use, and other misconduct. Common Dreams coverage of the launch put the starting set around 140 examples. Individual matters remain allegations or pending cases unless a court has ruled. Crossville already tracks several of the named local stories ( Chapman, Georgia, Greer, Pasadena, Baytown).

Spotlight · Staunton, VA · 2025-12-19 · Terminated · ~6 cameras

Staunton, VA, terminated after the CEO’s “coordinated attack” email

After months of resident opposition at council (a dozen speakers on Dec. 11), Flock CEO Garrett Langley emailed police clients that Flock and partner agencies were under a “coordinated attack” from activists who want to “defund the police, weaken public safety, and normalize lawlessness,” and that they were “trying to turn a public records process into a weapon.” Staunton Police Chief Jim Williams disagreed, and the city cancelled.

“Let’s call this what it is: Flock, and the law enforcement agencies we partner with, are under coordinated attack… from the same activist groups who want to defund the police, weaken public safety, and normalize lawlessness… They’re also trying to turn a public records process into a weapon against you and against us.”

Garrett Langley, Dec. 2025 email to police clients

“What we are seeing here is a group of local citizens who are raising concerns that we could be potentially surveilling private citizens, residents and visitors and using the data for nefarious purposes. These citizens have been exercising their rights to receive answers from me, my staff, and city officials, to include our elected leaders. In short, it is democracy in action.”

Staunton Police Chief Jim Williams, reply to Langley

The city said the CEO’s narrative “does not reflect the city’s values.” Contract terminated Jan. 8, 2026; cameras were later taken down. Williams later said the department was not actively exploring other stationary ALPR systems.
