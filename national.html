National · August 28, 2026 · USA TODAY search-log investigation

USA TODAY: reporters found Flock-search abuse before many agencies did

USA TODAY archived Aug. 28, 2026 reported August 28, 2026 that its reporters analyzed more than 75 million Flock searches spanning about three and a half years and more than 6,300 agencies. The newspaper said it flagged 240 users whose plate lookups stood out, then contacted 67 agencies. About half of those agencies confirmed or opened misuse investigations. USA TODAY said at least 19 police or sheriff’s-office employees in five states were arrested, fired, put on leave, or placed under investigation in the last two months after that reporting. This site does not treat those counts as proven misconduct. Arrests and charges are allegations.

The investigation said many of those searches used vague reasons such as “INVESTIGATION,” “TEST,” or a single letter. Where reporters could identify the cars, they said several plates belonged to people the searching employees knew. Cases already on this page that USA TODAY said it flagged first include Lufkin, Sarasota, Dallas, Georgia, and Cherokee County.

USA TODAY also reported that in December 2025 Flock changed monthly audit exports so most officer initials, plates, and search reasons are blacked out. Flock told the paper it made that change after a pattern of people using public audit data to identify undercover officers. When pressed for examples, USA TODAY said Flock did not provide any. Electronic Frontier Foundation investigator Dave Maass, quoted in the story, called the redactions catastrophic for public oversight and for agencies trying to audit searches run by outside departments. Flock said any officer abuse is unacceptable and that people who abuse the system should be held accountable.

Spotlight · Brookfield, Wisconsin · August 2026 · stale Flock homicide alert / high-risk stop

Brookfield PD holds SUV occupants at gunpoint after an outdated Milwaukee Flock alert

On August 6, 2026, around 4:46 p.m., a Flock Safety camera flagged a vehicle on Interstate 94 in Waukesha County as linked to a Milwaukee homicide with a suspect believed to be armed. CBS 58 archived Aug. 11, 2026 reported that Brookfield officers then ran a high-risk stop on westbound I-94 and ordered two people out of the SUV at gunpoint. Investigators quickly determined the occupants were not the people Milwaukee police were looking for.

Milwaukee PD had already cleared the vehicle from the investigation but had left the alert live. An MPD spokesperson told local stations, including TMJ4 archived Aug. 8, 2026, “This was not a Flock issue. MPD staff inadvertently did not remove a vehicle alert from the system.” Brookfield Police Chief Christopher Garcia defended his officers in a statement covered by WTMJ archived Aug. 9, 2026, saying they stopped the vehicle that was the subject of the alert. The occupants were released after Milwaukee confirmed it no longer wanted the car or the people.

The Milwaukee Journal Sentinel archived Aug. 26, 2026 reported August 25, 2026 that the driver retained a lawyer and is preparing a notice of claim against Milwaukee. No lawsuit is reported as filed in the cited coverage. This site does not name the civilian. ACLU of Wisconsin analyst Jon McCray Jones, quoted by CBS 58, called the episode a systemic failure: shared Flock data led another jurisdiction to stop an innocent person at gunpoint.

Spotlight · Staunton, VA · 2025-12-19 · Terminated · ~6 cameras

Staunton, VA, terminated after the CEO’s “coordinated attack” email

After months of resident opposition at council (a dozen speakers on Dec. 11), Flock CEO Garrett Langley emailed police clients that Flock and partner agencies were under a “coordinated attack” from activists who want to “defund the police, weaken public safety, and normalize lawlessness,” and that they were “trying to turn a public records process into a weapon.” Staunton Police Chief Jim Williams disagreed, and the city cancelled.

“Let’s call this what it is: Flock, and the law enforcement agencies we partner with, are under coordinated attack… from the same activist groups who want to defund the police, weaken public safety, and normalize lawlessness… They’re also trying to turn a public records process into a weapon against you and against us.”

Garrett Langley, Dec. 2025 email to police clients

“What we are seeing here is a group of local citizens who are raising concerns that we could be potentially surveilling private citizens, residents and visitors and using the data for nefarious purposes. These citizens have been exercising their rights to receive answers from me, my staff, and city officials, to include our elected leaders. In short, it is democracy in action.”

Staunton Police Chief Jim Williams, reply to Langley

The city said the CEO’s narrative “does not reflect the city’s values.” Contract terminated Jan. 8, 2026; cameras were later taken down. Williams later said the department was not actively exploring other stationary ALPR systems.

Developments

  2. Dec. 19, 2025: Langley emails police clients about a “coordinated attack”; the city says that narrative does not reflect its values.
