Jurisdiction

City vs county: who owns what

Crossville is the county seat. County residents shop and drive through town. That does not mean County Commission can cancel the city’s Flock contract, and it does not mean City Council can bind the Sheriff.

City of Crossville

  • Documented Flock quote Q-00234 (Crossville Police / city program).
  • Only City Council can cancel that city contract and remove city cameras.
  • Nov. 3, 2026 municipal election and Flock scorecard: Election 2026.

Cumberland County

  • This site has not found a published County Commission or Sheriff Flock, Axon, or other ALPR contract. As far as public records show, the county does not run its own.
  • Ask Commission to ban Flock, Axon, and other ALPRs.
  • Ask the Sheriff whether CCSO can search Crossville (or other) Flock reads, and to refuse that access where the law allows.

“Has not found” means no public county document turned up in this site’s local check, not a proof that no internal deal, sharing agreement, or unpublished quote exists. Send documentation to info@crossvilleprivacy.org.

The ask

What to ask Commission and the Sheriff

Ask first: ban Flock, Axon, and other county ALPRs. Cumberland County does not appear to run its own Flock, Axon, or other plate-reader system (this site has not found a county contract). Lock that in with a clear no. Don’t wait for a grant to pay for it.

  • Will Commission pass a recorded ban (resolution or ordinance) against buying, leasing, funding, or operating Flock, Axon, or any other automated license-plate reader or similar AI mass-surveillance tool for the county or Sheriff’s Office?
  • Confirm in writing: does Cumberland County, the Sheriff’s Office, or any county department have any Flock, Axon, or other ALPR agreement, quote, grant, or cooperative purchase today? Publish yes or no with the document.
  • Can CCSO officers search Crossville PD Flock reads (or any other agency’s network)? Will the Sheriff refuse that access where the law allows, and publish who can search what?
  • Will the county refuse any plate-reader “replacement” or pilot (switching vendors is not a privacy win)?
  • If any system ever exists, will Commission and the Sheriff publish retention under TCA § 55-10-302 archived May 5, 2025 and any federal / out-of-county sharing?

East Tennessee pattern: other counties are already fighting the same tools. See Knox County, Sullivan County, and Anderson County on the Tennessee page.

Speak

Public comment starter

Use Comments by the General Public at Commission. Keep it short. Name the ask.

I live in Cumberland County. As far as public records show, the county does not run its own Flock or ALPR cameras. Crossville’s city cameras can still scan ordinary county drivers on some routes into town, with no warrant and no suspicion. Not every way in or out is covered yet. Please ban county purchase or use of Flock, Axon, or any other plate-reader or similar AI mass-surveillance tool. Ask the Sheriff, on the record, whether CCSO can search Crossville or other Flock networks, and refuse that access. Switching vendors is not a win.

Quick answers

FAQ

Does Cumberland County have its own Flock or ALPR cameras?

This site has not found a published County Commission or Sheriff Flock or other ALPR contract. As far as public records show, the county does not run its own. The ask is to ban those tools. See What to ask.

Does Cumberland County run the Flock cameras in Crossville?

No. The documented Crossville Flock quote (Q-00234) is a City of Crossville / Crossville Police matter. Only City Council can cancel that contract. County drivers can still be scanned on some routes into town; not every way in or out is covered yet.

When does County Commission meet?

The County Clerk’s 2026 notice archived Aug. 26, 2026 schedules regular meetings at 6:00 P.M. in the Cumberland Room at Art Circle Public Library, 3 East Street, Crossville. Remaining 2026 dates on that notice include Sept. 21, Oct. 19, Nov. 16, and Dec. 21. Confirm the posted agenda before you go. Jump to meeting times.
