You do not have to make it easy

You are not required to feed a private company a permanent profile of your movements. This page covers legal, practical tools for Crossville and Cumberland County residents to shrink the data Flock Safety collects, through civic action, digital tools, and everyday habits that disrupt the vehicle fingerprint Flock builds over time.

One principle first: every tactic here is meant to be lawful in Tennessee. Where a common internet idea (tinted plate covers, IR sprays, a bike that blocks your plate) is illegal here, we say so and point to T.C.A. § 55-4-110 and the legal line. This is not legal advice; re-check the linked law before you act.

Federal legislation · highest-leverage national track

There are bills in Congress that would starve Flock-style systems

H.R. 9800, the Protection Against Mass Surveillance Act (Rep. Tim Burchett, TN-2, July 21, 2026), names Flock Safety directly. It would bar federal agencies from buying, operating, accessing, or contracting for ALPRs and similar mass-tracking systems, and block state and local use of federal funds for them. Improperly collected data would have to be deleted and kept out of court. Status as of late July / early August 2026: introduced and referred to committee; not yet law; needs cosponsors. Background: Tennessee · Burchett / H.R. 9800.

Related pressure: Rep. Keith Self’s H.R. 9716 PRIVACY Act (warrant for federal ALPR access) and Rep. Thomas Massie’s teased bill to cut federal funds for Flock-style cameras (national writeup). Flock has argued Burchett’s bill would effectively ban its cameras (Star News Network, Aug. 1, 2026).

Section 1 · Why this is your right

You do not owe a private company your movement data

Flock Safety is not a government agency. It is a venture-backed private company that profits by building databases of vehicle movement patterns and licensing access to them. When Flock’s cameras read your plate, they collect information about where you go, how often, and at what time, without your consent, without a warrant, and without any obligation to tell you what they do with that data long-term. Product context: Who is Flock · Flock dossier.

Choosing to reduce the data you hand them is not obstruction of justice. It is not anti-police. It is a reasonable privacy interest in your own daily movements. The Fourth Amendment has never required you to make it easier for surveillance systems, public or private, to track you. Documented personal-use abuse of the same kind of system already exists in Tennessee (Oak Ridge / Gauthier) and nationwide (misuse dossier · JSON).

Vehicle fingerprinting

Flock does not only read your plate number. Cameras capture make, model, color, body style, roof rack, bumper stickers, damage, and other visual attributes, building a unique profile over time. Each read adds to that profile. The more consistent your vehicle looks, the more accurate the fingerprint. Practical counter-tactics: §5. Sister explainer: Maryville counter-Flock guide.

“Confusing the algorithm”

The system works best when your vehicle looks identical at every pass. Small, lawful changes to your visual profile, over time and in rotation, make consistent pattern-matching harder. You are not hiding your vehicle. You are free to look different from one day to the next. Stay on the right side of T.C.A. § 55-4-110.

Crossville assessment: civic action is always the most powerful tool (§2). Tactics that reduce data collection are meaningful, but the camera network only keeps growing if it goes unchallenged. Both tracks matter. Local facts: Atlas · 11 cameras · Quote Q-00234 · home page.

Attend and speak during public comment

File a Tennessee Public Records Act (TPRA) request

Under TPRA, you can request the current Flock contract and addenda, retention / use policies, training materials, audits, sharing lists, camera inventories, outcome stats, and federal-access records. Be specific; vague asks get vague replies.

HOAs, neighbors, and Flock’s consumer apps

Flock markets to HOAs. If your association considers cameras, show up before the vote and ask who owns the data, retention, and police sharing. Most neighbors do not know the cameras exist; sharing this site or a printable card starts conversations that reach council. Do not use Flock’s consumer neighborhood products; they feed the same company. National cancel context: cancellations dossier.

Section 4 · Home cameras

Ring + Axon: the porch pipeline (and why Axon swaps matter)

While Flock reads plates on the street, Ring doorbell footage can be solicited into Axon’s evidence ecosystem. In 2025, Axon and Amazon’s Ring announced a partnership letting agencies request Ring footage routed through Axon’s Evidence system (Axon blog). Participation is framed as optional, but footage that leaves a home for a vendor’s servers can be stored and shared under police/vendor rules.

Amazon’s Ring later canceled a separate planned partnership with Flock Safety (Feb. 2026 reporting, summarized on our dossier), but the Axon–Ring pathway remains wherever agencies run Axon Evidence, including nearby Knoxville’s stack. The practical risk for Crossville: canceling Flock and “switching” to Axon can keep mass plate scanning and keep a doorbell request channel in the same vendor family. Full writeup: Axon + Ring · Not just Flock · Maryville: Who is Axon?.

The key distinction is not whether a company will answer a valid warrant (all must). It is whether the company built an always-on software channel for police to request footage without a warrant pipeline you opted into. The local-storage doorbells and NVRs below have no known Axon / Flock “Community Requests” style portal (unlike Ring). Prefer local-only mode and skip optional cloud plans.

Local-storage doorbells & systems (examples)

  • Eufy (Anker) video doorbells: Marketed for local storage on a HomeBase hub and/or microSD, with no mandatory subscription for core recording. Optional cloud exists; skip it. Honest caveat: in 2022–2023, reporting showed some Eufy “local” products still phoned home more than marketing implied; set up carefully and treat the brand’s privacy claims as something to verify, not trust blindly. Still: no public Ring-style LE request portal found.
  • Reolink Video Doorbell (Wi-Fi or PoE): Direct microSD recording (often up to 256GB) plus optional Reolink NVR. No subscription required for local playback. Strong pick if you want a dedicated doorbell without Amazon’s Neighbors pipeline.
  • Amcrest AD410: Budget wired 2K-class doorbell with onboard microSD and RTSP/ONVIF so you can record to your own NAS / NVR / Home Assistant stack and minimize cloud dependence.
  • Geeni (Halo and similar): Subscription-free microSD recording (e.g. Halo 2K up to 256GB). Optional cloud; skip it. No public Axon/Flock request portal found. Halo product page.
  • TP-Link Tapo doorbell / cameras: Local microSD (some models advertise high card capacity); optional cloud. No LE partnership portal. Note: TP-Link has faced U.S. security scrutiny; prefer local-only mode.
  • Reolink / Lorex-style NVR kits: Multi-camera systems that write to a hard drive in your home. Highest control; higher upfront cost and setup than a single doorbell.

Usually skip for this criterion: Google Nest, Arlo, and Blink are cloud-first / subscription-oriented. Wyze can use a microSD card, but many features push cloud (Cam Plus) and Wyze has said it turns over cloud-held footage under legal process; treat it as weaker than the local-first list above unless you stay SD-only and refuse cloud plans. All options remain subject to a valid warrant for a physical search or for any cloud copy you enabled. Vendor-swap cities: Not just Flock · Axon sources.

Section 5 · Vehicle fingerprint

Disrupt the fingerprint, legally

Tennessee legal note: T.C.A. § 55-4-110 prohibits tinted or camera-blocking license plate covers, even if the plate remains readable. Anti-camera covers sold online are illegal to use here. A hitch rack with a bike that physically blocks your plate while driving is also a plate-obstruction problem. Everything below this line is intended to stay legal. Do not cover, obscure, or reduce the legibility of your actual rear plate. Also on the home FAQ: Quick FAQ · statute cite in sources.

Rotate bumper stickers and magnets

Visible stickers and magnets are fingerprint features. Rotate them in and out, or go blank for a stretch. Tennessee requires only a rear plate on passenger cars, so the front of the vehicle is often the most variable surface ALPR cameras see.

Legal: magnets and stickers are not plate covers. Pattern adapted from the Maryville vehicle section.

Rotate decorative front plates

No front plate is issued or required for ordinary passenger vehicles in Tennessee (T.C.A. § 55-4-110 context). A novelty / university / blank front plate you rotate seasonally changes the front profile cameras see. Keep the rear plate fully visible and unobscured.

Remove dealership stickers and logo frames

Dealer stickers and branded frames are free advertising and consistent markers. Removing them reduces identifying features. If you use a frame, it must not cover plate text, numbers, county sticker, or state name.

Replacement plate (~$10 at county clerk)

Tennessee allows a replacement plate request (Form RV-F1315301) through your county clerk for about $10 plus fees. A new number severs the old plate’s history in Flock’s matching; a new history will start. Do not use the lost/stolen section unless the plate was actually lost or stolen. Confirm current Cumberland County Clerk process and fees before you go.

Rotate roof racks and hitch accessories

A roof rack one month and none the next changes silhouette. Keep the rear plate fully visible: a bike that sits in front of the plate is a problem; an empty rack that does not cover the plate is not. Plate relocation brackets exist when you haul bikes.

Specialty or personalized plates

Tennessee offers many specialty designs and vanity plates through the Department of Revenue. A different plate face changes visual appearance even when characters stay similar. Personalization has an added fee at registration; check current Tennessee DOR plate information.

Section 6 · Awareness habits

Small habits that reduce your profile over time

Use the garage if you have one

A car inside a private garage cannot be read on arrival or departure the way a driveway car can. Pair with DeFlock route awareness.

Orient away from known cameras

When you can choose how to park near a known camera, face the plate away from the lens. Map first: Crossville DeFlock map.

Vary travel times on regular routes

Leaving 20 minutes earlier occasionally degrades time-of-day profiles (deny the inputs).

Skip Ring, Flock, and Axon consumer apps

Opting out is silent, free, and immediate. Do not volunteer extra feeds. Why Ring still matters: §4 · dossier.

Prefer ordinary exteriors

Unique damage, rare wraps, and one-of-a-kind accessories make fingerprinting easier. Ordinary is harder to uniquely tag. Legal fingerprint tactics: §5.

Tell one neighbor

Share crossvilleprivacy.org, the next council date, or a printable card. Talking to people nearby is still the most underestimated tool.

Section 6B · Deny the inputs

Every camera read is an input. Deny the input, and the pattern weakens.

Flock does not have to follow you. It waits. Each pass can record plate, vehicle fingerprint, location, and timestamp. Enough dots become a map of home, work, school, church, clinic, meetings. None of this hides you from a lawful, individualized investigation. It refuses to hand a private company, for free, a permanent record of a law-abiding life.

Input How you deny it
Plate at a fixed point Vary routes; use a garage; consider a replacement plate number (§5)
Vehicle fingerprint Rotate stickers, magnets, racks, front novelty plates (§5)
Timestamp pattern Leave at different times; change errand order
Plate tied to your name Skip plate-based parking apps when you can; do not post clear plate photos; limit connected-car sharing

Parking apps

Plate-based apps tie your plate to location, time, and a payment card. Prefer cash at the meter when you can. If you must use an app, guest checkout and delete saved vehicles afterward.

Connected-car sharing

Many late-model cars transmit location and driving data to the automaker. Turn off optional data sharing in the app/account settings; decline dealer “connected services” you do not need; think twice about insurance telematics dongles.

Toll / RFID tags

Keep toll and access tags in an RFID-blocking pouch when you are not on a toll road or at a gate that needs them.

Do not post your plate

Crop or blur plates before posting vehicle photos. Cheap, immediate, and it keeps your number out of open-source lookups.

Section 7 · Legal line

What is illegal in Tennessee (do not do these)

  • Tinted, smoked, or camera-blocking plate covers, even if characters stay “readable” (T.C.A. § 55-4-110)
  • Sprays, films, or products marketed to defeat ALPR cameras while the plate is displayed on a public road
  • Any object (bike, cargo, frame) that covers or obscures required plate text while driving

Civic action (§2), TPRA requests, route variation (§3), and the vehicle habits above (§5) are the legal path. This site is not legal advice. Statute: T.C.A. § 55-4-110 · also listed in sources.

Section 8 · FAQ

Quick answers

Is covering my plate legal in Tennessee?

No. T.C.A. § 55-4-110 prohibits camera-blocking covers. Use council / email, TPRA, and the legal vehicle habits on this page instead.

Will canceling Flock fix everything if the city hires Axon?

No. Several cities dropped Flock and then installed Axon (or another brand) plate readers. That changes the company name, not mass scanning. Ask council to cancel Flock, remove the cameras, and refuse any plate-reader replacement. Require warrants for specific lookups. See Not just Flock, the dossier, and Axon swap sources.
