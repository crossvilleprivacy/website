Bernie Sanders: “STOP FLOCK”

On August 18, 2026, Independent Sen. Bernie Sanders of Vermont posted on X archived Aug. 18, 2026: “The AI surveillance company Flock has installed nearly 100,000 cameras across America to track your every movement. Going to the doctor? Flock knows. Dropping your kid off at school? Flock is tracking you. Flock is always watching. STOP AI MASS SURVEILLANCE. STOP FLOCK.” The Washington Times archived Aug. 20, 2026 reported two days later that no bill in Congress would ban the cameras nationwide, and that Sanders has not filed a Senate ban bill. Tennessee Republican Rep. Tim Burchett already introduced H.R. 9800 to cut federal money. Four days after the post, Flock CEO Garrett Langley told Fox News archived Aug. 22, 2026 the country needs a privacy-safety “compromise.” Full writeup: National cases.

Josh Hawley: Senate Flock probe

On August 26, 2026, Sen. Josh Hawley (R-Mo.), chair of the Senate Judiciary Subcommittee on Crime and Counterterrorism, opened a formal investigation into Flock Safety’s collection, retention, and dissemination of AI camera data. His press release archived Aug. 27, 2026 demands documents from CEO Garrett Langley no later than September 8, 2026. Hawley wrote that Flock’s network spans more than 120,000 cameras and that “Congress never authorized the network your industry has built.” Full writeup: National cases. See also Burchett’s H.R. 9800 and Sanders on STOP FLOCK.

Lois Kolkhorst: Texas ALPR ban call

KBTX archived Sept. 2, 2026 reported that State Sen. Lois W. Kolkhorst (R) is calling for a ban on automated license plate readers and said the Texas Senate will take up the issue when the 90th Legislature convenes in January. Her post was titled “Get the Flock Out of Texas!” Full writeup: National cases. See also Greg Abbott’s funding pause.

Josh Shapiro: Pennsylvania Flock ban

Gov. Josh Shapiro (D) said August 28, 2026, that he would welcome bipartisan legislation banning Flock cameras across Pennsylvania, calling them “not something in the public interest,” per NBC10 Philadelphia archived Aug. 30, 2026 and Axios Philadelphia archived Sept. 2, 2026. He said Pennsylvania State Police do not use Flock. No statewide ban has passed. Full writeup: National cases.

Greg Abbott: Texas Flock funding pause

On August 28, 2026, The Texas Tribune archived Aug. 29, 2026 reported that Gov. Greg Abbott (R) ordered all state agencies to pause funding for Flock cameras. His spokesman said Texas agency money cannot be used for Flock, and that most city Flock funding still comes from the federal government. Local contracts and existing cameras stay unless cities or counties end them. That is a state-funding freeze, not a statewide camera ban or a warrant rule. A companion Tribune investigation archived Aug. 29, 2026 said a $1 auto-insurance fee has gone toward at least $30 million and about 3,200 Flock cameras. Full writeup: National cases.

Ron DeSantis: Florida ALPR overhaul

At an August 26, 2026 event at Florida International University in Miami, Gov. Ron DeSantis (R) told reporters that automated license plate readers—including Flock and other vendors—have grown “out of control” and risk turning Florida into a “surveillance state.” Florida Politics archived Aug. 28, 2026 and News4JAX archived Aug. 28, 2026 reported DeSantis urging a legislative overhaul—not executive action alone—and pointing to New Hampshire’s roadside-reader limits as a model. He drew a line between criminal investigations and tracking ordinary residents: “Go after criminals, great. … But surveilling just regular Floridians’ movements and how that data’s used — that’s a bird of a different feather.”

“You want to get in your car and you want to go down to Buc-ee's in St. Augustine. It's really none of the government's damn business if you're doing that.”

Gov. Ron DeSantis, August 2026, on tracking ordinary Floridians’ movements (Fox 49 News archived Sept. 3, 2026; FOX 35 Orlando archived Sept. 1, 2026)

DeSantis kept pressing the issue after the FIU event. On August 31, 2026, Florida DOT revoked all ALPR permits on state highway rights-of-way and ordered removal within 30 days. Full writeups: Aug. 26 remarks, FDOT removal order.

Al Cutrona: Ohio felony-misuse bill

In August 2026, Ohio Sen. Al Cutrona (R-Canfield) told the Columbus Dispatch archived Aug. 22, 2026 he will introduce a bill barring sale or sharing of plate-reader data with third parties, requiring deletion after 10 days, and making personal misuse a fourth-degree felony. Full writeup: National cases. See also Levi Dean’s competing draft.

Levi Dean: Ohio warrant / AI-ban draft

Ohio Rep. Levi Dean (R-Xenia) told The Statehouse News Bureau archived Aug. 23, 2026 he is drafting a separate measure requiring warrants for government ALPR searches and banning AI-assisted government surveillance cameras, while still allowing private cameras with limits on government use of their data. Full writeup: National cases. See also Cutrona’s felony-misuse bill.

Arthur Orr: Alabama statewide ALPR guardrails

Alabama Sen. Arthur Orr (R-Decatur) told AL.com archived Aug. 24, 2026 in August 2026 that the status quo on Alabama’s ~2,800 Flock cameras is “totally unacceptable” and that lawmakers must “tighten the screws” on plate readers. Orr, who passed a 2021 Senate ALPR bill that stalled in the House, said he plans to file new legislation after the Nov. 3 general election—with ban, restrict, or middle-path options on the table. Full writeup: National cases. See also Robert Stewart.

Robert Stewart: Alabama wants change

Alabama Sen. Robert Stewart (D-Selma) told WSFA archived Aug. 29, 2026 in August 2026 that he wants to see change on Flock / ALPR rules before the 2027 legislative session. Full writeup: National cases. Primary bill sponsor: Arthur Orr.
